Do I need a vehicle report?

If you are buying a used vehicle, the seller knows things about it that you do not. A report is how you check the parts of that story which were written down somewhere: how many keepers it has had, whether an insurer has written it off, whether money is still owed on it, and whether the mileage on the dashboard matches what was recorded at each MOT.

None of that replaces looking at the vehicle, or a mechanic's inspection. It tells you which questions to ask before you hand over any money, and sometimes it tells you to walk away.

What a clear report does not mean

A check that finds nothing means no record was found on the register we searched, on the day we searched it. Damage settled privately, a finance agreement that was never registered, or an accident nobody reported leave no trace anywhere. We say so in the report rather than letting a blank line read as a guarantee.

What we do not do

  • We do not claim any partnership with the DVLA, the DVSA or any government body.
  • We do not promise checks we cannot actually carry out for your vehicle.
  • We do not generate reports automatically, and we do not guess at missing data.
  • We do not sell, share or market to your details.
